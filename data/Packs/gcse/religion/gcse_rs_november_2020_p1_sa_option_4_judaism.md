## Q1.3 [4 marks]

Explain two ways in which belief in the mitzvot between man and man influences
Jews today.

---

## Q1.4 [5 marks]

Explain two Jewish beliefs about the covenant with Moses at Sinai.
Refer to sacred writings or another source of Jewish belief and teaching in your
answer.

---

– 1 mark
credited.
• Sinai covenant was between God and the Israelites / told to Moses (Exodus 19:3 – And Moses
went up to God).
• Exodus 19:3-6 – God tells Moses to remind the Israelites what God has done for them, and that
they are God’s chosen people – a ‘nation of religious leaders, a holy people’.
• Includes the law – Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:1-20) and 613 mitzvot; Moses seen as ‘great
law-giver’.
• At Sinai, Moses received the Ten Commandments carved on stone tablets (given twice) / the key
laws for the Israelites to follow / which encapsulate all mitzvot.
• At Sinai, Moses received the 613 mitzvot / which were recorded in the Torah / these cover all
aspects of life / Orthodox Jews (especially) see the keeping of these as crucial to the well-being
of each individual and the Jewish nation as a whole.
• God agreed to bless the Israelites (chosen people) / ensuring they always had enough food
through good crops etc / whilst they agreed to keep God’s laws (mitzvot) (Deuteronomy 11:13-
15).
• Failing to keep the covenant would lead to God’s anger / shown through weather which prevented
crops (Deuteronomy 11:16-17), etc.

**Arguments in support:**
• In today’s world, there is much injustice / everyone should work to right that / eg by helping the

**Arguments in support of other views:**
• There are three key principles / all must be equal.
• Jews are instructed to ‘do what the Lord requires of you: only do justice, love kindness and walk
humbly with God’ (Micah 6:8) / so justice cannot be the most important as it is part of a list.
• Loving kindness (chesed) is the most important / as this leads to physically helping anyone / and
it brings happiness to both giver and receiver / builds relationships / encourages justice /
underpins Tikkun Olam / anyone can carry out acts of loving kindness as they require no power
only positive attitude / ‘The world is built on chesed’ (Psalm 89:3) / Torah, service to God and
chesed are three pillars of the world (Pirkei).
• Tikkun olam is most important / as it is about reconciliation, healing the world / Tikkun olam
includes justice and chesed within its remit / they are the ways in which Jews can heal the world,
