# Y7 RS / RP Exam Visual Practice Study Book

This Study Book supports the Year 7 RP/RS exam structure:

- **Part 1:** multiple-choice knowledge check
- **Part 2:** one medium explanation paragraph
- **Part 3:** one longer argument essay

The pack focuses on the main units: **What is Truth?**, **Hinduism**, **Judaism** and **Life of Jesus / Christianity**.

## Visual Revision Maps

### Part 2A — Karma

![2A Karma visual map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_2a_karma.png)

**Memory chain:** Karma → actions → consequences → samsara → dharma → moksha

**Model answer:**

Belief in **karma** is important for many Hindus because it teaches that every action has a consequence. Good actions create good karma, while bad actions create bad karma. This affects a person’s future in **samsara**, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth. Because of this, many Hindus try to follow their **dharma**, which means their duty or right way of living. For example, they may try to be kind, honest, respectful and avoid harming others. Karma is important because it encourages Hindus to make good moral choices in daily life. It also gives meaning to suffering and fairness, because Hindus may believe that actions from this life or a past life can affect what happens in the future. Therefore, karma matters because it shapes how Hindus behave and helps them move closer to **moksha**, freedom from rebirth.

### Part 2B — Shabbat

![2B Shabbat visual map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_2b_shabbat.png)

**Memory chain:** Shabbat → rest → family meal → God as Creator → Jewish identity

**Model answer:**

**Shabbat** is important for Jewish identity and family life because it is a weekly day of rest, worship and togetherness. It begins on Friday evening and ends on Saturday evening. During Shabbat, many Jewish families stop normal work, share a special meal, light candles, say prayers and spend time together. This helps families remember that God is the creator, as in the Jewish creation story God rested on the seventh day. Shabbat is also important because it helps Jewish people keep their religious identity strong. Even if Jews live in different countries, many of them share this same weekly practice. It reminds them that they belong to the Jewish community and are connected to their history and faith. Therefore, Shabbat is not just a rest day; it is a practice that strengthens family, worship and Jewish identity.

### Part 2C — Resurrection

![2C Resurrection visual map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_2c_resurrection.png)

**Memory chain:** Resurrection → Jesus rose → victory over death → hope → eternal life → Easter

**Model answer:**

The **resurrection of Jesus** is important for Christians because it is the belief that Jesus rose from the dead after his crucifixion. Christians believe this shows that Jesus was not just an ordinary human teacher, but the Son of God. The resurrection shows victory over sin and death, which gives Christians hope that death is not the end. It is also important because it supports the Christian belief in eternal life with God. For many Christians, the resurrection gives meaning to Easter, which is the most important Christian festival. It also encourages Christians to trust God, forgive others and live with hope even during suffering. Without the resurrection, Christianity would lose one of its central beliefs. Therefore, the resurrection is important because it gives Christians faith, hope and confidence in God’s power.

## Part 3 Essay Visual Plans

### 3A — Religious beliefs are more important than religious practices

![3A beliefs and practices visual essay map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_3a_beliefs_practices.png)

**Memory line:** Roots before fruit: Karma roots Hindu life, Resurrection roots Christian hope, Shabbat shows Jewish belief in action.

**Simple judgement:** I partly agree. Beliefs give meaning to practices, but practices show belief in action.

**Model answer:**

I partly agree that religious beliefs are more important than religious practices, because beliefs are often the reason why religious people behave in certain ways. However, I do not fully agree, because practices are also important as they show belief in action.

In Hinduism, beliefs are very important because they shape how Hindus understand life and morality. For example, many Hindus believe in **karma**, which means that actions have consequences. Good actions lead to good karma, while bad actions lead to bad karma. This belief is connected to **samsara**, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth. Because of this, Hindus may try to follow their **dharma**, which means their duty or right way of living. This shows that belief can be very powerful because it affects how people live every day.

In Christianity, beliefs are also extremely important. Christians believe that Jesus was the Son of God and that his **resurrection** shows victory over sin and death. This belief gives Christians hope that death is not the end and that God offers eternal life. Without this belief, many Christian practices, such as prayer, worship and celebrating Easter, would lose much of their meaning.

However, religious practices are also important because they allow people to express and strengthen their beliefs. In Judaism, **Shabbat** is a weekly practice where Jewish families rest, worship and spend time together. It is not just an empty routine; it reminds Jews of God as creator and helps keep Jewish identity strong.

Overall, I think religious beliefs are slightly more important because they give meaning to the practices. However, practices are still very important because they help believers live out their faith in daily life. Therefore, beliefs and practices are closely connected, but beliefs come first because they explain why the practices matter.

### 3B — Science explains the world better than religion

![3B science and religion visual essay map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_3b_science_religion.png)

**Memory line:** Science gives the map. Religion gives the compass.

**Simple judgement:** I partly agree. Science explains how the physical world works, but religion explains why life has meaning.

**Model answer:**

I partly agree that science explains the physical world better than religion, because science uses evidence, experiments and observation. However, I do not fully agree, because religion can answer different kinds of questions, especially questions about meaning, purpose and morality.

Science is very strong when it explains how the world works. For example, science can explain the Big Bang, evolution, gravity, medicine and the human body using evidence. Scientific explanations can be tested and changed if new evidence is discovered. This makes science useful and reliable for explaining physical processes. In the “What is Truth?” topic, this links to the idea that some truths are based on evidence and proof.

However, religion often answers questions that science does not fully answer. Science may explain how the universe began, but religion may ask why the universe exists or what the purpose of life is. For many religious people, truth is not only about scientific proof. It can also be about meaning, values and how humans should live.

For example, in Hinduism, the belief in **karma** helps Hindus understand moral responsibility. Science cannot prove karma in a laboratory, but the belief gives meaning to people’s actions and encourages them to behave well. In Christianity, belief in Jesus’ resurrection gives Christians hope that death is not the end. This is not a scientific explanation, but it gives meaning, comfort and moral guidance.

Overall, I think science explains the physical world better than religion, but religion can explain meaning, purpose and morality better than science. Therefore, I only partly agree with the statement. Science and religion answer different types of questions, so one does not completely replace the other.

### 3C — Family and community are the most important parts of religion

![3C family and community visual essay map](/assets/religion/y7/rs-rp/rs_3c_family_community.png)

**Memory line:** Family is the table. Community is the circle. Faith is the heart.

**Simple judgement:** I partly agree. Family and community are very important, but personal belief also matters.

**Model answer:**

I partly agree that family and community are very important parts of religion, because many religious beliefs and practices are passed on through families and shared communities. However, I do not fully agree, because personal belief and relationship with God or the divine are also very important.

In Judaism, family and community are extremely important. One strong example is **Shabbat**. During Shabbat, Jewish families rest, eat together, pray and remember God as creator. This practice brings families closer together and helps Jewish children learn their religion from their parents. It also strengthens Jewish identity because Jews around the world share the same weekly pattern of rest and worship.

Jewish festivals also show the importance of family and community. For example, during **Passover**, Jewish families remember the Exodus, when God freed the Israelites from slavery in Egypt. The Passover meal helps Jews remember their history and teaches younger generations about freedom, suffering and God’s care.

Family and community are also important in Christianity. Christians often worship together in church, pray together and celebrate festivals such as Christmas and Easter. The church community can support people during difficult times and help them live according to Jesus’ teachings, such as loving your neighbour and forgiving others.

However, religion is not only about family and community. Personal belief is also very important. In Hinduism, a person’s own **karma** and **dharma** matter because each person is responsible for their actions. In Christianity, a personal relationship with God and belief in Jesus’ resurrection are central.

Overall, I think family and community are among the most important parts of religion, especially in Judaism, because they help pass on beliefs, traditions and identity. However, they are not the only important part. Personal belief, moral behaviour and worship are also essential. Therefore, I partly agree.

## Key Words to Memorise

|---|---|
| Karma | Actions have consequences |

| Dharma | Duty or right way of living |
| Moksha | Freedom from rebirth |
| Shabbat | Jewish weekly day of rest and worship |
| Passover | Jewish festival remembering the Exodus |
| Resurrection | Christian belief that Jesus rose from the dead |
| Objective truth | Truth that is true regardless of opinion |
| Subjective truth | Truth linked to opinion or personal experience |
| Evidence | Information used to support a claim |
| Meaning | The purpose or importance of something |

## Exam Method

### Part 2 paragraph method

Use **D-E-E-M**:

1. **Define** the belief or practice.
2. **Explain** why it matters.
3. Give an **example** of how it affects daily life.
4. Link to **meaning** for believers.

### Part 3 essay method

Use **I-P-E-C-J**:

1. **Introduction** — state your overall view.
2. **Point** — one reason supporting the statement.
3. **Evidence** — religious example.
4. **Challenge** — another side of the argument.
5. **Judgement** — final balanced conclusion.
